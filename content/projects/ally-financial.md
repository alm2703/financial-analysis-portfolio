## Executive Summary

Ally Financial is a digitally led financial-services company whose economics remain anchored in US automotive finance. This case study builds from the operating drivers—originations, portfolio yield, funding costs, credit losses, and capital—rather than treating the financial statements as a black box.

> Work in progress. Historical data collection and model architecture are underway. Forecast conclusions will be added only after the operating model is reconciled.

## Company Overview

The analysis maps Ally's core automotive finance franchise alongside deposits, insurance, and corporate finance. The objective is to understand how asset growth, net financing revenue, provision expense, and capital interact across the cycle.

## Business Model

The model separates earning assets, pricing, funding, and credit. This makes it possible to distinguish growth from margin, and normalized loss expectations from changes in portfolio mix.

## Industry Context

Key external variables include new- and used-vehicle prices, dealer inventory, consumer affordability, benchmark rates, unemployment, and recovery values.

## Historical Financial Performance

Historical statements will be standardized into a decision-useful analytical format. Reported results, management adjustments, and model-derived metrics will remain clearly separated.

## Key Operating Drivers

- Retail auto originations and average loan balance
- Portfolio yield and asset mix
- Deposit beta and wholesale funding cost
- Delinquency, net charge-offs, and reserve coverage
- Common equity Tier 1 capital and capital returns

## Financial Model

The supporting model will connect loan balances, net financing revenue, provision expense, the three financial statements, capital, and valuation. Inputs and formulas will be visually separated and accompanied by a methodology note.

## Forecast

Base-case assumptions will be stated explicitly and tied to observable operating or macroeconomic variables.

## Scenario & Sensitivity Analysis

Scenarios will test the combined effect of funding costs, origination volumes, credit normalization, and used-vehicle recovery values.

## Valuation

Valuation will use methods appropriate for a balance-sheet lender, with assumptions reconciled to capital generation and risk.

## Risks

Principal risks include consumer credit deterioration, adverse funding repricing, weaker recoveries, residual-value exposure, and regulatory capital constraints.

## Key Findings / Management Insights

Findings will focus on the small number of operating variables that explain the largest changes in earnings, capital, and valuation.

## Data Sources

Primary sources will include company filings, earnings materials, regulatory data, and relevant industry datasets. Dates and transformations will be documented.

## Supporting Files

Model, cleaned data, and methodology files will be published here as each component is completed.
